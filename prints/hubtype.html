What kind of building

Is the hub an institution, an industrial building, a conversion, or something new on an empty lot? It is two buildings joined: a workshop, which is industrial, in a low building that already stands near the houses; and a community centre, which is civic, added along the street from salvaged parts.

The answer, in section

Schematic, not to scale; the programme and where each part sits are first thoughts, not a layout. No building, site or operator is chosen. The rated wall separates an industrial occupancy from assembly and mercantile ones, which also makes the two halves legible.

Workshop

The slow work that does not need the house's site: testing and regrading wood, taking components apart by hand, repair, sorting by lifespan and condition, and storage. A clear span, a roller door, a yard for a lorry, an enclosed room. In code terms, industrial.

Community centre

What a multiplex block lacks: rooms neighbours share. A café and a grocery, rooms for culture and exhibition, the shop that offers the parts, and a classroom that looks into the work. In code terms, assembly and mercantile.

Reuse first

The workshop moves into a store, garage or warehouse already there; the front is added from salvaged parts, so the hub shows what it does in how it is built. Where nothing stands, it is built new as a kit on the same plan. Both halves come apart: when a neighbourhood's houses have turned over, the front moves on and the shed goes back to another use.

Why reuse wins on carbon

The Preservation Green Lab's The Greenest Building found that reusing an average building almost always beats demolition and a more efficient new one, a saving that shrinks when a renovation needs many new materials; here the addition is a light front of salvaged parts. The City lists adaptive reuse first among its ways to keep building waste out of landfill.

Why a plain shed

Stewart Brand's “low road” buildings, plain and cheap, are the ones their users change most freely; old warehouses are his recurring example. A hub's work changes with what arrives each week, so it belongs in a low-road building, not a precious one.

Why not under housing

Vancouver delivers many community rooms in the base of new towers. A workshop with saws, lorries and stored wood sits badly below homes, and it would tie the hub to a developer's timing.

What reuse centres occupy

Almost every reuse centre I found lives in a building it did not build. None joins a workshop to a neighbourhood community centre: that pairing is the proposal.

To check with the City

Zoning

Five sites are C‑2A and one M‑1. C‑2 asks commercial uses to stay within a completely enclosed building, which suits indoor work. A salvage workshop is not clearly any listed use (“Recycling Depot” excludes a junk yard or shop), so the first step is the Director of Planning's discretion; a rezoning may still be needed.

Building code

Mixed major occupancies: assembly (A‑2) for the classroom and rooms, mercantile (E) for the shop, industrial (likely F‑2) for the workshop and its stored wood. Table 3.1.3.1 sets the separations, to confirm with a code consultant; a classroom that looks into the work needs rated glazing.

Interim use (option D)

No single interim-use policy found; CD‑1 by-laws allow interim uses case by case if compatible, easy to remove, low in cost and intensity, and time-limited, in one 2022 draft to five-year permits.
