Good afternoon. I'm Ruijie, and my project is Temporary Material Configurations: pause what architecture can do with houses Vancouver takes down for denser housing.

A permanent object can only be destroyed.

We build most houses as permanent objects: glued, sealed and poured together, so at the end pause the only way out is the excavator.

Buildings make about a third of the world's carbon.

UNEP / GlobalABC, Global Status Report for Buildings and Construction 2024/2025, 17 March 2025: 34% of global CO₂ emissions and 32% of energy; a sector headline, not a demolition-only share.

Why now? pause I'd start with carbon: the UN Environment Programme finds that buildings and construction make about a third of the world's emissions.

And about a third of what our region landfills.

Metro Vancouver, Construction and Demolition Waste: construction, renovation and demolition material is about a third of what goes to its landfill; 2022 waste composition study: wood and plastic are its largest parts. Drawing: R. Liu with Astra 6.

Closer to home, building and demolition material makes up about a third of what Metro Vancouver landfills.

Both studies led to one outcome. pause From observation on site and the online research database, I built the reuse decision framework. It asks: pause can this part do this job, here?

First, identify: a passport of what we know about each part, and what we don't.

Then evaluate it for that use: what it is, its carbon, whether it comes out whole.

Then decide, weighing evidence against the consequence of failure.

And design, least change first, a path into the next building, its record travelling with it.
